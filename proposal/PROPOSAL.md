# Proposal

## Problem Significance

Every e-commerce return forces a judgment call, legitimate or fraudulent? and getting it wrong is costly in both directions. Return fraud and abuse account for roughly 9% of all returns (NRF & Happy Returns, 2025), while online return rates are climbing toward 19.3% of sales. Retailers are already responding with friction — 72% now charge for at least one return method — but that friction backfires: 57% of shoppers say they'll stop shopping with a retailer after being charged for a return, up sharply from 40% a year earlier. This is not a problem a static rules engine can solve: return evidence is inherently unstructured (photos, free-text reasons, order history), the honest/fraudulent line is a nuanced judgment rather than a threshold, and 85% of merchants already use *some* AI/ML for fraud detection — yet only 45% consider it truly effective. The gap isn't a lack of automation; it's a lack of automation that reasons over multi-modal evidence, explains itself, and is governed enough that a human can be accountable for every denial. That combination is inherently AI-native: it requires synthesizing weak, conflicting signals into a single explainable decision, which is exactly what a single rule or classifier cannot do alone.

## Prior Work & Gaps

Our design draws on eight papers spanning agentic reasoning, grounding, and human oversight. Du et al. (2023) and Gou et al. (2024) show that decomposing reasoning across multiple roles — and adding a dedicated critic that revises against external, tool-grounded evidence rather than pure self-reflection — measurably outperforms single-pass LLM judgment, motivating our Planner → Decision → Critic structure rather than one monolithic "decide" call. Lewis et al. (2020) established retrieval-augmented generation as the standard mitigation for hallucination in knowledge-intensive tasks and, critically, gives generated answers provenance — the retrieved passage that supports a claim — which is what makes a policy verdict auditable rather than just plausible-sounding. On the human side, Lai et al. (2021) and Visser et al. (2023) converge on a finding existing fraud tools ignore: oversight only works if it's genuine, not rubber-stamped, and appropriate reliance requires a reviewer to see *why*, not just *what*. Tax et al. (2021) frames fraud detection as an organizational, adversarial problem — not just a classification task — reinforcing that ReturnGuard needs investigator-facing audit trails, not just accuracy metrics. Kholkar and Ahuja (2025) is the closest prior system to our governance layer, converting written policy into enforceable LLM guardrails with human-in-the-loop approval — but their own results (policy-extraction Macro-F1 as low as 24.5%, enforcement accuracy ~70–73%) show this is unsolved, motivating our second governance layer (OPA fail-closed checks) rather than trusting a single guardrail pass. Existing commercial fraud tools fall short precisely where this literature points: they're black-box scores with no retrieval grounding, no critic step, and no mandatory human checkpoint on denials.

## Proposed Technical Approach

ReturnGuard is a governed multi-agent LangGraph pipeline. A submitted return flows through Data-Quality Gate → Planner → Intake → Policy (RAG, grounded per Lewis et al.) → Image (CLIP matching + AI-image detection) → Behavior (risk model) → Decision → Critic (tool-grounded re-verification per Gou et al.) → Explanation → Governance Gate. Approvals can flow through automatically; every denial is held for mandatory human confirmation, informed by Lai et al. and Visser et al.'s finding that oversight requires real evidence, not a rubber stamp. OPA enforces fail-closed policy checks and ContextForge governs tool orchestration at the MCP level, forming the second guardrail layer inspired by, but going beyond, Kholkar and Ahuja (2025).

## Checkpoint 2 Validation Plan

We will validate: (1) Decision-agent accuracy against a hand-labeled set of synthetic and adapted real-world-style return cases; (2) Critic catch-rate by injecting known reasoning errors into Decision outputs and measuring what fraction the Critic flags; (3) Policy RAG retrieval precision against our actual policy corpus; (4) Governance Gate correctness — verifying every simulated deny routes to human review with zero silent auto-denials.

## Risk Analysis & Mitigation

**Privacy/security:** minimize PII in agent context; encrypt data at rest; scope MCP tool access via least privilege. **Bias/toxicity:** audit the Behavior risk model for disparate impact across customer segments before deployment; log every feature contributing to a risk score. **Safety:** OPA fails closed on any policy-check error, defaulting to human review rather than auto-approval. **Hallucination:** every Policy verdict must cite its retrieved passage (per Lewis et al.), and the Critic agent re-verifies Decision claims against actual Policy/Image/Behavior outputs before Explanation generation.
